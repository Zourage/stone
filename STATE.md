# STATE — read this first, every session

Current state of the language and the method, kept short on purpose. `decisions.md` is the append-only history with rationale; read the entries cited here when you need the why. When anything changes: append to `decisions.md`, then update this file and the spec it touches.

## The language
- Purpose: asking, code, discussion, engineering and science talk between two people; the corpus is its codex. (D1, D34)
- Script: 74 syllable glyphs as Hangul blocks that spell their own sound (initial + vowel, no final), `spec/codepoints.json` from `scripts/compose_hangul.py`; sounds in `spec/phonology.md`. 1–3 tokens per glyph. Priors accepted in D52 and now tested four times: acceptance tests 3, 4 and 5 and the first drift test all found no Korean reading and no slide toward Korean. (D28, D37, D52, D86, D98)
- No Latin form of any word exists anywhere. IPA describes sounds only. (CLAUDE.md rule 3)
- **Word order is closed and enforced at both levels: subject, n-times, object, adverbials, verb.** The subject comes first, always (131 to 13 in the corpus, the 13 corrected under D102 and D103). An n-times phrase precedes the object, because counting material precedes what it counts (9 to 0; D48, D97). Every other adverbial follows the object and stands immediately before the predicate — time, place, while, until/since, because, before/after, for, from, and the with-phrase of manner, instrument or comitative. Every type is unanimous after D103. The with word coordinating two arguments into a subject is part of the subject, so it precedes the object (15 lines, all pronoun + pronoun + with). `validate.py` **errors** on a violation of either rule. (D78, D87, D97, D98, D102, D103)
- One open root class; roots are neither noun nor verb. Verb template: root, negation, tense, evidential, stance, subordinator; only the evidential is mandatory. Arguments bare, or root plus one case marker (object, location, possessor). **A marker follows the word it marks: no case marker, relational word or question particle opens a sentence or follows another marker, and or/but stand between what they join (D59, D104).** A pronoun, the interrogative, a numeral or a root before a root is a determiner or compound; the personal pronouns are not determiners, because possession takes the possessor case. No adjectives, no adverbs. (D26, D29–D33, D44, D48, D64, D102, D104)
- Evidentials, obligatory: direct, reported, inferred, general. Stance, optional: intend, predict, propose, trust, risk, assert. Tense: past, present unmarked, future (temporal only). Irrealis clauses (conditions, modal complements) take the general evidential — checked over the whole corpus at 0 violations (D101). A first-person intend predicate carries the future marker; the predict stance takes the inferred evidential. (D30, D31, D34, D60, D61, D88)
- Four modal roots share a syllable and one construction over a subordinate clause: able, want, should, allow. (D61, D63)
- Semantic carving, `lexicon/README.md`: knowing ×3, error ×3, asking ×3, change ×3; experiment/test/try = 1. Saying is one root; the message root is a noun. A person choosing takes the choose root; the condition/branch root is the branch point in code. Quantified duration takes the measure root, unquantified takes the time root. (D35, D90, D92, D95)

## Tools
- `scripts/translate.py`: gloss, check, lookup, examples need no key; to-english, to-stone, roundtrip need `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`. `scripts/stonelib.py` holds the parsing both it and the validator use. All three layers have been run against the live model. (D72, D76)
- `scripts/drift_test.py`: the drift test, both arms. Needs the key. (D74)
- `scripts/correct_corpus.py`: the reviewed route for correcting an existing corpus line; every correction needs a `why`, dry run by default. (D81)
- **A model-free translator, both directions** (D106, D107), wired into `translate.py` as `english` and `stone` (no key; `to-english`/`to-stone` remain the model-backed pair). `scripts/realize.py` is stone → English at 22.2% exact and 27 of 30 meaning-correct by hand; `scripts/compose.py` is English → stone at 40.4% exact (35.5% held), and it **declines with a reason** on another 24% instead of guessing. Missing English is treated as a phrase-building problem, not a dead end (D108): a word may be derived from a root (`slowly` → quality + the with word), or be a construction (`everyone`, `while`, `because`, `but`) — but one of the three carves the English cannot decide (`hold`, `choose`, `variable`) still declines, which is where this language and toki pona part company. `compose.py --roundtrip` composes and realises back, reporting drift as a confidence (0.69 mean where right, 0.44 where wrong — a signal, not a verdict), which is the seam a model would plug into. Three of the four steps are near-free because the corpus already forced them: fixed cues (D84–D105), a near-functional lexicon, and word order closed (D102, D103). The fourth, English parsing, is ordinary NLP. **Neither is a substitute for the acceptance test** — both were written from the spec, so they say nothing about whether a cold reader could reconstruct the language.
- `realize.py --ambiguous`: the 64 sentences (11 held) where `[root][root][marker]` is structurally undecidable (G34).
- `scripts/regression_test.py`: re-runs every check in `validate.py` against the pre-correction form of all 62 corrected lines, from `tests/fixtures/pre_correction.jsonl` — 28 fronting, 17 object-order, 6 rule, 2 leak, 1 collision, and 11 corrections no check can see. Run it beside `validate.py`. (D101–D103)
- The guardrail is a template check, not a meaning check: it catches a broken verb ending, a non-word, and since D104 a marker with nothing to mark; it does **not** catch a wrong case marker. `check`'s modal advisory is narrower than it sounds — it fires when the complement is a bare root, and says nothing when a complement keeps the general evidential but drops the subordinator, or carries the wrong evidential (D76, D101). Read the parser-produced gloss, not the verdict.
- See `scripts/README.md` for what each script does.

## The method
- `corpus/` is the deliverable; spec and lexicon are scaffolding. Done = a cold session with only the train corpus translates held-out sentences both ways. (D2, D3)
- Acceptance test: the two directions go to **different** agents, so no agent sees both sides of a held sentence (D75). And a held sentence may not be a copy of a train sentence, which is the same leak one level up — the validator now fails on it (D93). Check the held set before the run, not only through the validator.
- **A run's number describes the corpus it was run on.** Changing the *train* split changes the measurement even when no held line moves, because what the test measures is reconstruction from the train split. D102 and D103 both said test 5 was "unaffected" because they touched no held line; that was the wrong test, and 43 of the 888 pairs those agents read have since changed or been added (D104).
- Drift test: two arms, the long session and the fresh-session control. A run without the control is not interpretable. (D74)
- Words enter only via `scripts/validate.py --add`; run `scripts/validate.py` and `scripts/regression_test.py` before every commit. Multi-syllable forms are rejected if they are Korean words (`data/ko_frequency.json`) or if they read as a shorter root plus a verb ending. (rules 4, 6; D55)
- Batches: one subagent per domain with a disjoint first-syllable pool; `scripts/merge_batch.py BATCH_DIR` dry-runs, the maintainer reviews, `--apply` writes. It only appends. (D58, D66)
- **The full treatment for a word-order gap, done three times and it works:** count every sub-type rather than the ones the gap was logged under, choose the rule the counts already support and say why, correct every violating line through `correct_corpus.py` with the attested parallel named in each `why`, write the check, show it firing on the pre-correction forms and silent on the corrected corpus, put those forms in the fixture — and then mutation-test it. (D78, D102, D103, D104)
- **Two ways to test a check, and they answer different questions.** `regression_test.py` proves it catches the defects that *were* in the corpus. **Mutation testing** — take the held sentences that exercise a rule, mutate each into a violation, require the check to fire — proves it catches defects nobody happened to write. Do both when a check is added or widened, and record the score; `tests/README.md` has the protocol and how to read a non-catch. (D82, D101, D104)
- **An exemption only the check knows about is the same defect as a convention only the prose knows about.** The cue check ran as "any marked slot carries a cue" where the rule says "every", which was right for 24 lines because D88 pins the predict stance to the inferred evidential — an exemption in force everywhere and written nowhere — and wrong for the one line that hid behind it (D105).
- **A check earns its count by what it enumerates, what scope it walks, and what it has been made to miss.** Three sessions running the gap was in the check, not the rule: shapes it never asked about (D102), clauses it never walked (D103), a marker it was told to ignore for a reason that had expired (D104). The gloss-collision report is the standing example — it cannot see the say/message carve it was built for, nor the D95 choose carve, because both live in `sense_notes` prose while it reads the `gloss` field.
- `validate.py` **errors** on: an adverbial before the main-clause subject (D78, D102); an adverbial before the object rather than after it (D103); a marker with nothing to mark (D104); a held sentence whose script or English is verbatim in train (D93); a first-person intend predicate without the future marker; the predict stance on the direct evidential; **every** marked evidential or stance whose English carries none of its own cues, with three exemptions — the imperative is the intend cue, an irrealis protasis needs no general cue, and the predict stance supplies the inferred evidential (D84, D88, D91, D94, D105); and `corpus/README.md`'s cue sentence or feature-tag list drifting from the code and the corpus (D101). It **reports**: English words naming more than one predicate root, baseline 1 — "hold", where transitivity separates the two (D94).
- **The acceptance test is not a linter.** Test 4 cost ~683k subagent tokens and eleven of its twelve misses were findable by static audit; that is what the checks above are for. Spend a run on the question only it can answer. (D94)
- Four places a defect hides, all four found by hand rather than by a miss: a convention that lives only in prose (D82, D87); **a carve the lexicon states and the corpus contradicts** (D95); **a rule the spec states and the corpus contradicts** (D99); and **a rule the spec states, the corpus mostly follows, and a check appears to enforce** (D101–D104). Read the corpus counts, not the prose, and read what the check actually looks at.
- Codepoints: BMP PUA is stripped by Claude's input pipeline (D20); Yi Syllables trip a content classifier on the chat surface (D52). Neither is usable.
