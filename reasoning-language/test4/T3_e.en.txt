[heading] Section (namely proof) 1.
1. [definition] W1(A) is defined as: that return sum of the 1st to the A-th odd numbers.
2. [recalled] For each A in all positive integers: the A-th odd number is equal to subtract(multiply(2, A), 1).
3. [plan] I prove that for each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).
4. [it follows from 2] The 1st odd number is equal to 1.
5. [it follows from 1, 4] W1(1) is equal to 1.
6. [recalled] Multiply(1, 1) is equal to 1.
7. [it follows from 5, 6] W1(1) is equal to multiply(1, 1).
8. [suppose] The number (call it B) is a positive integer, and W1(number B) is equal to multiply(number B, number B).
9. [it follows from 1, 2; under the supposition of 8] W1(add(number B, 1)) is equal to add(W1(number B), subtract(multiply(2, add(number B, 1)), 1)).
10. [recalled; under the supposition of 8] Subtract(multiply(2, add(number B, 1)), 1) is equal to add(multiply(2, number B), 1).
11. [it follows from 8, 9, 10; under the supposition of 8] W1(add(number B, 1)) is equal to add(multiply(number B, number B), add(multiply(2, number B), 1)).
12. [recalled; under the supposition of 8] Multiply(add(number B, 1), add(number B, 1)) is equal to add(multiply(number B, number B), add(multiply(2, number B), 1)).
13. [it follows from 11, 12; under the supposition of 8] W1(add(number B, 1)) is equal to multiply(add(number B, 1), add(number B, 1)).
14. [it follows from 8, 13] For each B in all positive integers: if W1(number B) is equal to multiply(number B, number B), then W1(add(number B, 1)) is equal to multiply(add(number B, 1), add(number B, 1)).
15. [recalled] If the statement (call it C) is true about 1, and for each D in all positive integers: if statement C is true about D, then statement C is true about add(D, 1), then statement C is true about all positive integers.
16. [it follows from 7, 14, 15] For each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).
[heading] Section (namely function) 2.
[definition] Sorted connection(E, F) is defined as: let G be []; let H be 0; let J be 0; while H is less than count(E), and J is less than count(F): if item(E, H) is more than item(F, J), then let G be append(G, item(F, J)); let J be add(J, 1); otherwise let G be append(G, item(E, H)); let H be add(H, 1); while H is less than count(E): let G be append(G, item(E, H)); let H be add(H, 1); while J is less than count(F): let G be append(G, item(F, J)); let J be add(J, 1); return G.
