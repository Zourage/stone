[heading] Section (namely proof) 1.
1. [definition] W1(A) is defined as: that return sum of the 1st to the A-th odd numbers.
2. [plan] I prove that for each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).
3. [recalled] For each A in all positive integers: the A-th odd number is equal to subtract(multiply(2, A), 1).
4. [it follows from 1, 3] W1(1) is equal to 1.
5. [recalled] Multiply(1, 1) is equal to 1.
6. [it follows from 4, 5] W1(1) is equal to multiply(1, 1).
7. [suppose] W1(B) is equal to multiply(B, B) about any positive integer (call it B).
8. [definition] Let C be add(integer B, 1).
9. [it follows from 3, 8; under the supposition of 7] The C-th odd number is equal to add(multiply(2, integer B), 1).
10. [it follows from 1, 8; under the supposition of 7] W1(C) is equal to add(W1(integer B), the C-th odd number).
11. [it follows from 7, 9, 10; under the supposition of 7] W1(C) is equal to add(multiply(integer B, integer B), add(multiply(2, integer B), 1)).
12. [it follows from 8; under the supposition of 7] Multiply(C, C) is equal to add(multiply(integer B, integer B), add(multiply(2, integer B), 1)).
13. [it follows from 11, 12; under the supposition of 7] W1(C) is equal to multiply(C, C).
14. [it follows from 7, 8, 13] For each B in all positive integers: if W1(integer B) is equal to multiply(integer B, integer B), then W1(add(integer B, 1)) is equal to multiply(add(integer B, 1), add(integer B, 1)).
15. [recalled] If the statement (call it D) is true about 1, and for each A in all positive integers: if statement D is true about A, then statement D is true about add(A, 1), then statement D is true about all positive integers.
16. [it follows from 6, 14, 15] For each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).
[heading] Section (namely function) 2.
17. [definition] “sorted connection” means merge.
18. [definition] Sorted connection(E, F) is defined as: let G be []; let H be 0; let J be 0; while H is less than count(E), and J is less than count(F): if item(E, H) is not more than item(F, J), then let G be append(G, item(E, H)); let H be add(H, 1); otherwise let G be append(G, item(F, J)); let J be add(J, 1); while H is less than count(E): let G be append(G, item(E, H)); let H be add(H, 1); while J is less than count(F): let G be append(G, item(F, J)); let J be add(J, 1); return G.
[heading] Section (namely reason) 3.
19. [claim; explaining 18] The function takes the smallest item from all items that are not taken yet per the step of the loop because E and F have been sorted.
20. [it follows from 19] Any item that is already inside G is not bigger than any item that will be joined to G per the step of the loop.
21. [it follows from 20; as a result of the previous sentence] G has been sorted at the end of the function.
