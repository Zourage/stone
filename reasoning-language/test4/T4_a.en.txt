[heading] Section (namely proof) 1.
1. [plan] I prove that for each A in all positive integers: the sum of the 1st to the A-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(A, add(A, 1)), 2).
2. [given] The sum of the 1st to the 1st positive integers is equal to 1.
3. [given] Divide(multiply(1, add(1, 1)), 2) is equal to 1.
4. [it follows from 2, 3] The sum of the 1st to the 1st positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(1, add(1, 1)), 2).
5. [suppose] The number (call it B) is a positive integer.
6. [suppose] The sum of the 1st to the B-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(number B, add(number B, 1)), 2).
7. [recalled; under the supposition of 5] The sum of the 1st to the (addition number B 1)-th positive integers is equal to add(sum of the 1st to the B-th positive integers, add(number B, 1)).
8. [it follows from 6, 7; under the supposition of 5, 6] The sum of the 1st to the (addition number B 1)-th positive integers is equal to add(divide(multiply(number B, add(number B, 1)), 2), add(number B, 1)).
9. [recalled] Add(divide(multiply(number B, add(number B, 1)), 2), add(number B, 1)) is equal to divide(multiply(add(number B, 1), add(add(number B, 1), 1)), 2).
10. [it follows from 8, 9; under the supposition of 5, 6] The sum of the 1st to the (addition number B 1)-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(add(number B, 1), add(add(number B, 1), 1)), 2).
11. [it follows from 5, 6, 10] For each B in all positive integers: if the sum of the 1st to the B-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(number B, add(number B, 1)), 2), then the sum of the 1st to the (addition number B 1)-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(add(number B, 1), add(add(number B, 1), 1)), 2).
12. [recalled] If the statement (call it C) is true about 1, and for each B in all positive integers: if statement C is true about number B, then statement C is true about add(number B, 1), then statement C is true about all positive integers.
13. [it follows from 4, 11, 12] For each A in all positive integers: the sum of the 1st to the A-th positive integers is equal to divide(multiply(A, add(A, 1)), 2).
[heading] Section (namely proof) 2.
14. [suppose] The prime biggest number (call it D) exists.
15. [it follows from 14; under the supposition of 14] The prime set of numbers is finite.
16. [it follows from 15; under the supposition of 14] The number that is equal to add(prime product of all numbers, 1) (call it E) exists.
17. [it follows from 16; under the supposition of 14] For each F in all prime numbers: remainder(number E, F) is equal to 1.
18. [it follows from 17; under the supposition of 14] The prime factor of number E does not exist.
19. [recalled] For each G in all integers that are more big than 1: the prime factor of G exists.
20. [it follows from 16; under the supposition of 14] Number E is bigger than 1.
21. [it follows from 19, 20; under the supposition of 14] The prime factor of number E exists.
22. [it follows from 18, 21; under the supposition of 14] Step 18 is contradict step 21.
23. [it follows from 14, 22] The prime biggest number does not exist.
