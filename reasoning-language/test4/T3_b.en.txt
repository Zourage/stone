[heading] Task 3.
[heading] Proof.
1. [definition] W1(A) is defined as: sum of A odd positive smallest numbers.
2. [recalled] For each B in all positive integers: the B-th odd positive number is equal to subtract(multiply(2, B), 1).
3. [it follows from 1, 2] W1(1) is equal to 1.
4. [it follows from 1, 2] For each A in all positive integers: W1(add(A, 1)) is equal to add(W1(A), subtract(multiply(2, add(A, 1)), 1)).
[heading] Theorem.
5. [claim] For each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).
6. [it follows from 3] W1(1) is equal to multiply(1, 1).
7. [suppose] The arbitrary number (call it C) is a positive integer.
8. [suppose; under the supposition of 7] W1(number C) is equal to multiply(number C, number C).
9. [it follows from 4, 7; under the supposition of 7, 8] W1(add(number C, 1)) is equal to add(W1(number C), subtract(multiply(2, add(number C, 1)), 1)).
10. [it follows from 8, 9; under the supposition of 7, 8] W1(add(number C, 1)) is equal to add(multiply(number C, number C), subtract(multiply(2, add(number C, 1)), 1)).
11. [recalled] For each A in all integers: add(multiply(A, A), subtract(multiply(2, add(A, 1)), 1)) is equal to multiply(add(A, 1), add(A, 1)).
12. [it follows from 7, 10, 11; under the supposition of 7, 8] W1(add(number C, 1)) is equal to multiply(add(number C, 1), add(number C, 1)).
13. [it follows from 7, 8, 12] For each A in all positive integers: if W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A), then W1(add(A, 1)) is equal to multiply(add(A, 1), add(A, 1)).
14. [recalled] If the statement (call it D) is true about 1, and for each A in all positive integers: if statement D is true about A, then statement D is true about add(A, 1), then statement D is true about all positive integers.
15. [it follows from 6, 13, 14; restating 5; certain] For each A in all positive integers: W1(A) is equal to multiply(A, A).

17. [definition] “[]” means an empty list.
18. [definition] “append” means a function that puts the item to the end of the list.

[definition] Sorted connection(A, B) is defined as: let F be []; let number C be 0; let G be 0; while number C is less than count(A): if G is less than count(B), then if item(A, number C) is not more than item(B, G), then let F be append(F, item(A, number C)); let number C be add(number C, 1); otherwise let F be append(F, item(B, G)); let G be add(G, 1); otherwise let F be append(F, item(A, number C)); let number C be add(number C, 1); while G is less than count(B): let F be append(F, item(B, G)); let G be add(G, 1); return F.
20. [rule] A and B must be W2S.
[heading] Explain product.
21. [definition] “W3” means an item of A or B that the function does not put to F yet.
22. [it follows from 19, 20] Item(A, number C) is always smallest than all W3S of A, and item(B, G) is always smallest than all W3S of B.
23. [it follows from 19, 22] The function puts the W3 that is smallest than all W3S per the step to the end of F.
24. [it follows from 23] Any item of F (call it E) is not smaller than any item of F that is before item E, and so F is a W2.
