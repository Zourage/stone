[heading] Hodgkin Huxley model.
1. [recalled] Hodgkin and Huxley published the model (call it A) about the squid giant axon in year 1952.
2. [recalled] Model A describes the membrane of the axon (call it B) as the electric circuit.
3. [recalled] Membrane B behaves like the capacitor, and the capacitance of membrane B is equal to 1 uF/cm2.
4. [recalled; explaining 3] Membrane B separates the ion that is inside the axon from the ion that is outside the axon, and so membrane B stores the charge.
5. [recalled] The ion passes through the channel of membrane B, and the conductance is equal to the easeness of this passing.
6. [recalled] Model A has 3 conductances (namely gNa for the sodium, and gK for the potassium, and gL for the leak).
7. [recalled] The maximum of gNa is equal to 120 mS/cm2, and the maximum of gK is equal to 36 mS/cm2, and gL is equal to 0.3 mS/cm2.
8. [recalled] GL constants, but gNa and gK are depending on the time and the voltage of membrane B (call it C).
9. [recalled] The Nernst equation computes the nernst potential of a certain ion from 2 concentrationnesses of the ion (namely concentrationness that is inside the axon and concentrationness that is outside the axon).
10. [recalled] If voltage C is equal to the nernst potential of the ion (call it D), then the current of ion D is equal to 0.
11. [recalled] ENa (namely nernst potential of the sodium) is equal to 50 mV because the sodium is more concentrated outside the axon, and EK (namely nernst potential of the potassium) is equal to -77 mV because the potassium is more concentrated inside the axon.
12. [recalled] Model A uses the equation (namely C*dV/dt = I - gNa*m^3*h*(V - ENa) - gK*n^4*(V - EK) - gL*(V - EL)) (call it E).
13. [recalled; detailing 12] V is representing voltage C, and C is representing the capacitance of membrane B, and I is representing the input current.
14. [recalled] 3 gating variablenesses (namely m, and h, and n) are a number that is between 0 and 1, and all gating variablenesses are representing the chance such that 1 door of the channel is open.
15. [recalled] The sodium channel has 3 doors m and 1 door h, and the potassium channel has 4 doors n.
16. [recalled; as a result of 15] GNa*m^3*h is equal to the conductance of the sodium, and gK*n^4 is equal to the conductance of the potassium.
17. [recalled] All gating variablenesses follow the equation (namely dx/dt = (x_inf(V) - x)/tau_x(V)).
18. [recalled] If voltage C rises, then m increases in a fast way, and n increases in a slow way, and h decreases in a slow way.
[heading] Action potential and refractory period.
19. [recalled] Voltage C is equal to about -65 mV at the rest state because m is very small.
20. [recalled] If the input current raises voltage C to above the threshold (namely -55 mV), then m increases in a fast way, and so the sodium enters the axon, and so voltage C rises more.
21. [recalled; detailing 20] This process is a positive loop because the rise of voltage C increases m, and the increase of m raises voltage C.
22. [recalled; as a result of the previous sentence] Voltage C rises to about 40 mV for fewer than 1 ms, but voltage C does not reach ENa.
23. [recalled; next after the previous sentence] H decreases, and n increases, and so the sodium stops entering the axon, and the potassium leaves from the axon, and so voltage C descends.
24. [recalled; next after the previous sentence] Voltage C descends to below the rest value, and voltage C is near EK because n is still big.
