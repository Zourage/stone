[heading] Catalytic loop of the Suzuki Miyaura cross coupling.
1. [recalled] Akira Suzuki and Norio Miyaura published the reaction that is a cross coupling (call it A) in year 1979.
2. [recalled] Reaction A joins 2 carbons of kind sp2 with bond C-C, and reaction A uses the palladium as the catalyst.
3. [recalled; as an example of 2] 4 Bromotoluene (call it B) and the phenylboronic acid (call it C) react to 4 Methylbiphenyl (call it D).
4. [recalled; detailing 3] The reaction of step 3 uses the amount that is 1–5 mol% of Pd(PPh3)4, and the base such as K2CO3 (call it E), and the solvent that is a water and a toluene at the temperature that is about 80 °C.
5. [recalled] Reaction A uses PPh3 (namely triphenylphosphine) as the ligand of the palladium.
6. [recalled] For each F in all ligands PPh3: F gives 2 electrons to the palladium.
7. [recalled] The rule that is 18 electrons says that the complex of the metal that has 18 valence electrons is very stable.
8. [recalled] Pd(PPh3)4 has 18 electrons because Pd(0) has 10 electrons of kind d, and 4 ligands give 8 electrons.
9. [it follows from 7, 8] Pd(PPh3)4 is stable, but Pd(PPh3)4 is not active as the catalyst.
10. [recalled] Pd(PPh3)4 loses 2 ligands before the loop, and so Pd(PPh3)2 with 14 electrons (call it G) appears.
11. [recalled] G is active because the empty place exists at G.
[heading] Step 1 (namely oxidative addition).
12. [recalled] G enters bond C-Br of B, and so the complex (namely Ar-Pd(II)-Br) (call it H) appears.
13. [recalled] Ar means the 4 Tolyl group.
14. [recalled] The oxidation state of the palladium changes from 0 to 2 because the palladium gives 2 electrons to 2 new bonds.
15. [recalled; adding to 14] The tally of the electron changes from 14 to 16.
16. [recalled] Step 1 is often a slowest step.
17. [recalled; adding to 16] Step 1 is faster about bond C-I than about bond C-Br, and step 1 is faster about bond C-Br than about bond C-Cl.
18. [recalled; explaining 17] The energy of bond C-Br of the aryl bromide is equal to about 335 kJ/mol, and the energy of bond C-Cl is equal to about 400 kJ/mol.
[heading] Step 2 (namely transmetalation).
19. [recalled] Step 2 moves the phenyl group from the boron to the palladium, and the phenyl group enters complex H in place of the bromide.
20. [claim] Many students believe that base E only deletes the acid that the reaction makes, and step 2 does not need base E.

[heading] Step 3 (namely reductive elimination).
29. [recalled] 2 groups of complex K must cis before step 3.

31. [recalled] Step 3 joins 2 groups with new bond C-C, and so D appears, and the palladium returns to G.

[heading] Side reaction (namely beta hydride elimination).
