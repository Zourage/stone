# Scope items. Each item is an English sentence that can be read two ways. For EACH reading (A and B) write one sentence of the constructed language that has exactly that reading and cannot be taken as the other. So 24 sentences in all, with ids 1A, 1B, 2A ... 12B.

1. "I did not leave because of the rain." A: I stayed, and the rain was the reason. B: I did leave, but the rain was not the reason.
2. "You must not open the valve and start the pump." A: What is forbidden is doing both; doing only one of them is allowed. B: Each of the two actions is forbidden by itself.
3. "Probably the test failed and the log was deleted." A: The speaker finds it probable that both things happened; neither is stated as fact. B: The test probably failed; that the log was deleted is stated as plain fact.
4. "You do not have to attend / you must not attend." A: Attending is not required (you may stay away or come). B: Staying away is required (you are forbidden to attend).
5. "He did not answer deliberately." A: He chose on purpose to give no answer. B: He answered, but the answering was not done on purpose.
6. "We can not test the old version." A: We are free to skip testing it (it is possible for us not to test it). B: We are unable to test it.
7. "The engineer does not believe the bridge is safe." A: The engineer believes that the bridge is unsafe. B: The engineer has no belief that it is safe (the engineer may simply have no opinion).
8. "I hope the test passes and the build is fast." A: The speaker hopes for both things. B: The speaker hopes the test passes, and states as a fact that the build is fast.
9. "The alarm did not ring twice." A: On two occasions the alarm failed to ring. B: It is false that the alarm rang two times (it rang some other number of times).
10. "She did not speak to anger him." A: She stayed silent, and her purpose in staying silent was to anger him. B: She spoke, but angering him was not her purpose.
11. "The guard must check the bags or the tickets." A: The guard has one duty with a free choice: check at least one of the two. B: The guard has one specific duty, either the bags or the tickets, and the speaker does not know which.
12. "He was not sad and tired." A: He was not sad; he was tired. B: It is false that he was both sad and tired at once.
