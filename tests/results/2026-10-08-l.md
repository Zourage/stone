Corpus 1477, lexicon 230, at `5333716`. **Serves goal 1** (D125).

A/B — the same 116 held sentences, the same four models, two train splits differing

worse. The real finding is that a 120-item draw cannot adjudicate D50's bar.**

## The finding that matters more: the bar is not adjudicable at this sample size

| held items | 1 SE | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 3.9 pts | ±7.6 |
| 120 | 2.7 pts | ±5.4 |
| 250 | 1.9 pts | ±3.7 |
| 464 | 1.4 pts | ±2.7 |

**A 120-item draw cannot separate 87% from 93%.** D50's bar is 90%, and every run from
test 3 onward has reported a pass or a miss against it from a single draw of 107–125
items. Test 9 said three of five models clear it; test 10 said one of four. **Same
corpus, same models, different verdict, from the draw alone.**

This does not overturn test 5's pass — 97.5% sits more than two standard errors clear
of 90%. It does mean that **any run landing between roughly 85% and 95% on ~120 items
is a result consistent with both passing and failing**, and tests 7 (89.6%), 8, 9 and
10 are all in that band. Those runs measured the corpus; they did not adjudicate it.

**What to do instead:** report a confidence interval alongside the point estimate,
and treat a cross-draw difference under ~5 points as noise unless it is paired. The
paired design used here costs one extra arm and removes the variance entirely, because
every held item is its own control.

## Verdict

1. This week's 43 lines slightly **helped** (+1.3, net +6 items), measured by pairing.
2. Test 9 against test 10 is noise. Neither run's model ordering should be leaned on.
3. **D50's bar needs an interval, not a point.** Single 120-item draws have been
   over-read for eight runs.
4. The recoining holds; the GLM harness bug recurred and the fix must live in the
   protocol, not in one script.
