## Protocol

Eight cold agents. Each was given the 888 train sentences as `English ||| the script` pairs and nothing else — no spec, no lexicon, no grammar — and asked for the translations, the rules it inferred, and whether the language is a known one.

Each agent made **exactly two tool calls**: one read of its own self-contained prompt file outside the repo, and one write of its answer file. Each was instructed in terms to ignore the project `CLAUDE.md` in its context, which tells any session to read `STATE.md` first, and to touch nothing under the repo root. All eight confirmed unprompted that they read no repository file, ran no code and made no web request. This is test 4's protocol unchanged, and it is the closest a subagent can come to the toolless ideal: the prompt is 57 KB, too large to hand over inline, so one read of it is the irreducible cost. What matters for the measurement is what was *in* the prompt, and that was the train pairs and nothing else.

**The split.** The 121 held sentences were cut into four contiguous chunks in corpus order — 31 / 30 / 30 / 30 (s0021–s0256, s0269–s0503, s0511–s0769, s0777–s0987). Chunk *k* went to two agents: one was shown only the script side and asked for English, the other only the English side and asked for the script. Every held sentence got both directions and **no agent ever saw both sides of one sentence** (D75). Same boundaries as test 4, deliberately, so the per-chunk comparison is direct.

### Class A — a real model miss (1 miss)
**s0022** "I tested it with you."
- gold: `아 우 파 에 하 와타카` — I | it-OBJ | you with | test-PAST-DIR
- agent: `아 에 하 우 파 와타카` — I | you with | it-OBJ | test-PAST-DIR

The with word does two different jobs and the corpus separates them by position, cleanly and without a single exception:

- A **genuine adverbial** with-phrase — comitative, instrument or manner — stands **after** the object. 24 train sentences, no counterexample: s0419 "I discussed this with you", s0783 "I trust you with this task", s0393 and s0791 "I'll help you with the fix/review", s0765 "Share the data with me", s0420 and s0792 "Discuss it with me", s0167, s0435, s0603, and every manner phrase (fast, slowly, easily, and the how-question).
- The with word used to **coordinate two arguments into a subject** stands before the object, because it is part of the subject. 16 train sentences, no counterexample — and every one of the 16 has a plural English subject: "we", "you and I", "that one and I".

So the split is functional and total: 24–0 one way, 16–0 the other. The English of s0022 is unambiguous — a singular subject and an explicit comitative — and the nearest train parallel, s0419 "I discussed this with you", has exactly the gold order. The agent produced instead the verbatim script of train **s0515 "We tested it."**

The corpus determines this one and the agent got it wrong. That is a real model miss and it should be recorded as one. It is not a corpus defect: nothing was contradictory, nothing was thin, and the contrast pair the agent needed was sitting in its prompt.

It does not change meaning. "We tested it" with an inclusive we and "I tested it with you" pick out the same two participants and the same event; the agent chose a different construction, not a different fact. Worth knowing, though, that in a clause with **no** object the two uses of the with word are genuinely indistinguishable — held s0357 `아 우 하 두하루카무` is rendered "I don't agree with that" while train s0870 `아 우 하 두하카` is rendered "That one and I agree", the same shape read both ways. That costs nothing for symmetric predicates like agree, which is all the corpus uses it on, so it is an ambiguity to record rather than a gap to close.

### Class B — thin coverage (1 miss)

- gold: `에 야 수포 메루 우 파 데타 추` — you | how many times | it-OBJ | read-PAST Q
- agent: `에 우 파 야 수포 메루 데타 추` — you | it-OBJ | how many times | read-PAST Q

An n-times phrase stands before the object: 6 train sentences, no counterexample (s0519, s0521, s0616, s0676, s0713, s0794). The agent put the object first.

But the phrase here is the **interrogative** n-times phrase, and that combination had **no train example at all**. Getting this right meant composing two patterns neither of which was demonstrated together, and by the criterion `tests/README.md` states — a construction with no train sentence is a corpus gap, not a model failure — that makes it thin coverage.

Three train sentences added (below), none with a second-person subject, so s0522 still requires the generalisation rather than being handed it.

This is the same sentence that missed in test 4, and the miss has moved one slot inward: then the agent misplaced the **subject** relative to the n-times phrase, which is what D78 governs and D87 enforced; now the subject is correct and only the object placement is wrong. D87 worked and uncovered a finer question underneath it.

### Class C — an undecided point (1 miss)
**s0529** "The new version is smaller than the old one."
- gold: `파캐 파테 파캐루코디 파테 호 테카` — new version | [not-new-SUB] **version** from | small-DIR
- agent: `파캐 파테 파캐루코디 토 호 테카` — new version | [not-new-SUB] **thing** from | small-DIR

An English anaphoric "one" carrying a modifier is rendered two ways in train and nothing states which to use:

- **Repeat the head root**: s0120 "The small test is done; the big one is not" repeats the test root; s0630 "you take the heavy one" repeats the part root.

But the rule was never written down, it has two examples per side, and both of the thing-root examples have an English shape ("the small one") indistinguishable from s0529's "the old one". The agent picked an attested rendering and was marked wrong by an undecided point.

Logged as **G30** and decided in D95: state the latent rule, since it is what the corpus already does. Four train sentences added, two on each side.

## What the hand analysis found that no miss showed

Reading the anaphora evidence turned up a defect that cost nothing this run but would have cost later. English **"choose"** mapped to two different roots in train with nothing in the English to tell them apart: s0295 "Choose one." and s0296 "I chose the small one." used the **condition/branch** root, while s0400, s0401, s0402 and s0427 used the **choose** root. The lexicon already decides it — w0100's sense notes say in terms that a person choosing takes the choose root, and that the condition root is the branch point in code — so the two lines contradicted the lexicon, not merely each other.

This is the say/tell defect of D90 exactly: two roots, one English word, the English → stone direction undetermined. It escaped four acceptance tests because no held sentence happens to use "choose". Both lines corrected under D94. Correcting them dropped the condition root to one train sentence, so three sentences were added to restore it.

The second thing the reading turned up is larger than any of the three misses, and it is a defect in the **spec**, not the corpus. Tracing s0022 meant checking what `spec/grammar.md` says about order, and since D78 it has said that an adverbial "follows the subject **and precedes the object**". The first half is right and is enforced by the validator. **The second half is false.** The corpus puts a time or place adverbial **after** the object in 22 train sentences and before it in 3; it puts a comitative, instrument or manner with-phrase after the object in 24 with no counterexample; and it splits 5 to 3 on the while-clause. It contradicts itself outright on time adverbials of the same kind — s0639 "Don't run the test at night" puts the phrase before the object, s0114 "Run it now" after it.

So the spec has been asserting the minority pattern as the rule since D78. The spec is scaffolding and the corpus is authoritative, so under **D98** the false sentence is removed and replaced by what is actually settled: the subject rule, the n-times rule, and the with-phrase rule.

**Where a time or place phrase and a while-clause sit relative to the object is logged as G31 and deliberately left open.** It is the same kind of gap D78 closed one level up and it deserves the same treatment — full counts, a rule chosen for a reason, every violating line corrected through `correct_corpus.py`, and a validator check then shown to catch the pre-correction forms. Eight to eleven lines move. Tacking that onto the end of a test run is precisely how D78 came to be half applied, and D87 is the record of what that cost; so it is the next session's work on its own. It cost nothing measurable here — the majority patterns are strong enough that the agents learned them, and neither order miss turns on G31 — but a corpus that teaches both orders for a common adverbial is exactly what cost test 3 six misses, so it is logged loudly rather than buried.

The third thing the reading turned up is a genuine structural ambiguity, found independently by the chunk-2 agent and flagged in its own write-up without being asked. Held **s0277** `타푸 니 파 도타카` "The function returned three." has two well-formed parses, because a numeral after a root is a label (s0824 "Do step three" is `캐다 니 파`, the identical shape):

- `[타푸] [니 파] [도타카]` — the function, as bare subject, returned three.
- `[타푸 니 파] [도타카]` — "function three" as object, agentless, so: function three was returned.

Both readings are available and nothing in the script chooses. It did not cost a miss in either direction — the agent took the intended reading and said so — and the English → stone item scored exact, so the direction the bar measures is determined. Recorded as a standing language ambiguity alongside the true/hold one, not closed: closing it would mean marking the ordinal, which is a change to the language for one sentence's sake.

## Priors check (D52)

All eight agents were asked directly. **All eight said constructed, not a known language**, and all eight named the script as the only real-world element — Hangul used as a bare open-syllable CV syllabary with no final consonants, which several flagged as unlike Korean. **No agent translated any word by its Korean reading.** Several ruled out near-misses explicitly: that the case markers are not the Korean particles, that the numerals do not line up, that the lexicon contains no Korean morphemes. Typological comparisons offered were Korean/Japanese/Turkic head-finality, Quechua/Tibetan/Turkic evidentials and Papuan/Austronesian quinary numerals — all statements about type, none about vocabulary. Several named the single-vowel antonym grid and the software-testing semantic field as what settled it.

Mechanical leakage check on the production direction, run the way `drift_test.py` runs it: of the 594 script tokens the four agents emitted, 331 coincide with an entry in `data/ko_frequency.json`, and **all 331 are readable as words of this language** — they are the one-syllable function words and pronouns that collide with Korean by construction. Zero tokens are Korean words the parser cannot read, which is the failure D52 left open. Fourth surface in a row on which the D52 priors hold.

## Verdict

**The bar is met. Stone → English 121/121 meaning-correct; English → stone 118/121 = 97.5% exact against a ≥90% bar, with all three misses well formed and none changing meaning.**

This is the first run to pass, and it is worth being precise about what it means and what it does not.

What it means: **the corpus alone teaches the language.** Eight agents with no grammar, no lexicon and no spec — only 888 sentence pairs — reconstructed the system well enough to read 121 unseen sentences without a single meaning error and to write 118 of 121 in a form identical to the corpus's own, character for character, including obligatory evidential marking, the six stances, the verb-slot order, the case system, the nominalised modals and the quinary numerals. That is what D3 set out to test and the standard D50 set for it. The held set was valid by construction this time, the two directions never met, and the number was not helped by any item that answered itself.

What it does not mean: the language is not finished, and the run says nothing about the 31 features still under the D11 count of 10, about registers the corpus does not cover, or about how the language behaves in use rather than in translation — the drift test measures that separately and has been run once. One real model miss at 97.5% is also a reminder that the ceiling is not 100%: s0022 was determined 24–0 in the train split and still went wrong.

And the pass does not mean the corpus has no contradictions left. **G31 is open and it is a real one** — the corpus teaches both orders for a time or place adverbial relative to the object, 22 to 3, and for the while-clause, 5 to 3. That did not cost a miss because the majorities are strong, and a test at 97.5% can carry a latent inconsistency without showing it. The bar being met is a statement about what the corpus teaches, not a certificate that every line agrees with every other.

The honest summary of three runs: test 3 measured the corpus's contradictions, test 4 measured the ones test 3's fixes left behind, and test 5 measured the corpus. It passes.
