# Acceptance test 4 — 2026-09-15, the full held set, corrected corpus

Corpus s0001–s0992, 871 train, **121 held**. Lexicon 206 entries. Script Hangul.
Bar (D50): stone → English 100% meaning-correct, English → stone ≥90% exact, every miss read by hand and grammatical.

Run against the corpus as it stood after D78–D85 and **before** the corrections this run produced. Sizes above are the tested corpus; the corrections and additions made afterwards took it to 1009 lines.

## Protocol

Eight cold agents. Each was given the 871 train sentences as `English ||| the script` pairs and nothing else — no spec, no lexicon, no grammar — and asked for the translations, the rules it inferred, and whether the language is a known one. Each agent's only tool calls were one read of its own self-contained prompt file outside the repo and one write of its answers; each confirmed in its report that it never touched the repo.

**The split.** The 121 held sentences were cut into four contiguous chunks in corpus order — 31 / 30 / 30 / 30 (s0021–s0256, s0269–s0503, s0511–s0769, s0777–s0987). Chunk *k* went to two agents: one was shown only the script side and asked for English, the other only the English side and asked for the script. Every held sentence got both directions and **no agent ever saw both sides of one sentence** (D75). Contiguous rather than interleaved chunks, so that test 3's finding about corpus age stays readable.

## Stone → English: 121/121 meaning-correct — passes

Read by hand against the corpus English. 75 of 121 came back word-identical; the other 46 differ only in contraction, article, number-neutral rendering, deixis ("in this one" / "here"), or which of the fixed cues was used for the same evidential or stance. None changed a meaning.

Four of the differences are the agent being **more** faithful to the script than the corpus English is, and each one flags a defect on the English side:

- **s0547** came back "A slow test failing is better than a fast test", where the corpus English says "A slow test is better than a fast test that fails". The script nominalises the failing of the slow test with the subordinator, and **every one of the 60-odd subordinator-nominalised clauses in the corpus is a clause nominaliser; not one is a prenominal relative** — a relative clause takes the finite relativiser instead (s0019, s0073, s0324, s0335). The agent is right and the corpus English attached the relative to the wrong noun. Corrected under D91.
- **s0624** came back in the past, where the corpus English is in the present; both predicates carry the past marker. The agent is right. Corrected under D91.
- **s0629** came back as "It is difficult for a person to hold a heavy thing", correctly reading the generic-person subject that its twin s0835 does not have. The corpus English for the two was indistinguishable. Corrected under D91.
- **s0475** came back as "It cannot be faulty, I gather", supplying the inferred cue its corpus English omits. Corrected under D91.

This is the same signature test 3 saw, and it is worth naming: the stone → English direction is the corpus's own proof-reader. Every English-side defect this session found was found by an agent reading the script and writing down what it actually said.

## English → stone: 109/121 exact = 90.1% as scored — but see the leak below; it does not clearly clear the bar

Per chunk: 31/31, 26/30, 25/30, 27/30. Test 3's monotonic decline with corpus age is gone; the weakest chunk is now the middle of the corpus, not the newest part.

**On the 107 sentences test 3 also used: 96/107 = 89.7%**, against test 3's 87/107 = 81.3%. On the 14 held sentences added since: 13/14.

### Two of the 121 items were not valid test items

Found after scoring, by an audit this run prompted. **s0021** "It failed in this one." had the script of train s0111 "It failed here." verbatim, and **s0987** "That one and I agree." was identical to train s0870 in *both* the English and the script. Both scored exact. Neither measured anything: the answer was sitting in the agent's own prompt.

This is the D75 leak one level up. D75 stopped an agent seeing both sides of a held sentence; nothing stopped a held sentence from being a copy of a train sentence. Discounting the two, **English → stone is 107/119 = 89.9%**, a hair *below* the ≥90% bar.

So the honest reading of this run is: **English → stone sits at the bar, not above it.** Reporting 90.1% as a pass would be reporting a number that two invalid items produced. Both lines are corrected, and `scripts/validate.py` now fails on any held sentence whose script or English is verbatim in train (D93), so the whole held set is valid from here.

All 12 misses parse as well formed under `scripts/translate.py check`. **All 12 preserve the meaning**, against 17 of 20 in test 3. None is ungrammatical.

### Class A — the residue of D78, which was only half applied (3 misses)
**s0522, s0817, s0860.**

D78 closed G27 by putting the subject first and an adverbial after it, and rewrote fourteen lines. It left fourteen more untouched: the n-times phrase was never addressed (s0520, s0655, s0880, held s0522, held s0856) and neither was the subordinate adverbial clause in the because, until, since and while constructions (s0804, s0816, s0858, s0859, s0886, s0888, s0889, held s0540, held s0817). **s0858 is the exact line D78's own rationale named as the conflict with s0857, and it was not corrected.**

The three misses cut both ways, which is the signature of a corpus that still teaches both orders:
- **s0817** — the agent put the subject before the because-clause, as D78 requires; gold fronts the clause. The agent is right.
- **s0860** — the agent fronted the because-clause; gold has the subject first, as D78 requires. The agent is wrong, and it was taught to be wrong by s0816, s0886 and s0888.
- **s0522** — the agent put the subject before the n-times phrase; gold fronts the phrase. The agent is right.

Not a failure of D78. D78 is the right rule and it was under-enforced. The reason it rotted unseen is that **the validator's convention heuristic could not see any of it**: D82 added a check that only looked for a pronoun subject displaced past a phrase containing a location marker, a relational word or a subordinator, so every noun subject and every n-times phrase was invisible to it, and it reported 1 while 14 lines were wrong. Fixed under D87; the new heuristic catches 14 of 14 on the pre-correction forms.

### Class B — the corpus grading against its own 20:1 and 14:1 majority (2 misses)
**s0551, s0708.**

Both gold lines put a first-person "I'll …" intend predicate in the bare form with no future marker. Test 3 called this "attested both ways" on a count of 114 without against 28 with — but that count is dominated by imperatives, which take no tense. Restricted to first-person declaratives, **train is 20 to 1 for the future marker**, and the single exception is itself a defect. The agents supplied it in both cases and lost the match to the minority form.

s0551 misses twice over: its second predicate pairs the predict stance with the **direct** evidential, where train pairs predict with the inferred evidential 14 times against 1. The agent wrote the inferred. A prediction cannot rest on direct evidence, so the agent is right on the language as well as on the counts.

Corrected under D88, along with the two train lines (s0630, s0134) that taught the minority.

### Class C — one English sentence with two renderings, and one evidential with no cue (2 misses)
- **s0629** "Holding a heavy thing is difficult. That's just how it is." Its script carries the generic-person subject; held **s0835**, whose English is "Holding a heavy thing is difficult. Everyone knows that.", does not. `corpus/README.md` lists those two general cues as interchangeable alternates for the same evidential, so **nothing in either English distinguishes the two scripts** and the English → stone direction is undetermined. Two different agents in two different chunks produced the same script for the two Englishes, which is the proof. A D83 violation; corrected under D91.
- **s0475** "It can't be a fault." Gold carries the inferred evidential with no inferred cue in the English, and `corpus/README.md` says a cue-less sentence is direct. The agent wrote the direct evidential — it applied the stated rule. This is the **only** line in the corpus with a bare inferred evidential and no cue. Same failure shape as G28, one evidential over; corrected under D91, which extends the cue rule from the general evidential (D79) and the stance slot (D84) to every evidential.

### Class D — a lexical carve the corpus contradicted (1 miss)
**s0378** "Say it before the test." The agent used the message root as a predicate; gold uses the say/tell root. That looked like a plain model miss until the corpus was checked: **s0313 "The machine said this." and s0317 "What did the machine say?" use the message root as a predicate and gloss it "said"**, while the say/tell root glosses as "say, tell". Two roots, one English word, nothing to tell them apart. Corrected under D90.

This is the miss that comes closest to changing meaning, and it is still a corpus defect rather than a model failure.

### Class E — thin train coverage (3 misses)
- **s0893** "How many weeks will the task take?" Gold uses the measure root for a quantified duration. That carve — quantified duration takes the measure root, unquantified takes the time root — had **one** clean example (s0896) against eight for the bare time root, and the agent generalised from the eight. Six sentences added (D92).
- **s0405** "It has not started, I gather." Gold leaves the predicate unmarked; the agent added the past marker. The English present perfect of an *event* had **no train example at all** — the four "has been … since" lines are statives inside the since-construction. Five sentences added (D92).
- **s0540** "It failed while I was reading it." Gold marks the past inside the while-clause; the agent left it unmarked. Exactly **one** train sentence (s0804) shows a while-clause whose English subordinate is past; the other eight have a present subordinate and no marker. Three sentences added (D92). (s0540's gold also violates D78, but the agent reproduced that order, so it is not what caused the miss.)

### Class F — an undecided point (1 miss)
**s0431** "Did you run it, or did the machine?" Gold repeats the entire clause on both sides of the or word. No train sentence does that: all five train alternative questions coordinate just the differing predicate or complement, and **subject alternation had zero train examples**. The agent coordinated the two subjects and shared the rest, which is the better-supported reading. Logged and closed as **G29** under D89; s0431 and the one half-repeating train line (s0806) are corrected to the rule, and three train sentences now show subject alternation.

### Real model misses
**None.** Every one of the twelve traces to a corpus defect, an undecided point, or a construction with three or fewer train examples. Test 3 had four.

**Did D78–D85 move the number?** Yes: +8.4 points on the same 107 sentences, and the classes that dominated test 3 behave as intended.

- **G28 / the general evidential (test 3 class B, 3 misses, the only 3 that changed meaning): gone.** D79's cue rule holds. Not one miss in test 4 is a general evidential, and the stone → English agents used the general cues correctly throughout. This was the most important of the two decisions and it worked cleanly.
- **Defective held sentences (test 3 class C, 2 misses): gone.** s0750, s0746, s0860's construction and s0532 all pass now. D80 did its job.
- **Thin coverage (test 3 class D, 4 misses): gone, and replaced.** All four of test 3's thin constructions — we-exclusive, region nominals, how-often, clause-as-subject — pass in test 4. Three *new* thin constructions took their place, which is what one expects from a corpus that keeps growing into new territory rather than a defect in the method.
- **G27 / word order (test 3 class A, 6 misses): down to 3, but not because the rule is wrong.** D78 is the right decision; it was applied to fourteen lines and fourteen more were left. This is the one place where the session that acted on test 3 under-delivered, and the validator check that was supposed to prevent exactly this could not see the violations it was written for.
- **Minority-form gold (test 3 class E, 1 miss): grew to 2**, because test 3 mis-diagnosed it as "attested both ways" on a count that mixed imperatives into the first-person declarative construction. Corrected properly under D88.

The deeper change is in the *kind* of miss. Test 3 had 4 real model misses and 3 meaning changes. Test 4 has 0 and 0. Every remaining miss is the corpus failing to determine an answer it should have determined, and each one is now determined.

## Rules the agents inferred

Complete and correct, in all eight independent write-ups, and materially the same as test 3's. Between them: strict head-final SOV with prenominal modifiers; the case markers and relational words, including the from word doing ablative, comparison and cause, and the with word doing comitative, instrument and manner; the verb template root–negation–tense–evidential–stance–subordinator with the right gloss for all four evidentials and all six stances; the question particle with the evidential slot empty and the interrogative in situ; the whole interrogative series off one root; the imperative as second person plus intend; no copula; the finite relativiser against the clause nominaliser, and the nominaliser carrying all four modals, while, until, since, because, become and still; the conditional protasis in the general evidential plus the free conditional word; quinary numerals inside a decimal system; numeral before a root as a count and after it as a label.

Nothing was inferred wrongly. Several agents independently noted the vowel-gradation antonym pairs and the paradigmatic grids as evidence of design — a property of the language, not an error.

## Priors check (D52)

All eight agents were asked directly. All eight said constructed, not a known language, and all eight named the script as the only real-world element — used as a bare open-syllable CV syllabary with no final consonants, which several flagged as unlike Korean. **No agent translated any word by its Korean reading.** Several ruled out near-misses explicitly: that the case markers are not the Korean particles, that the numerals do not line up, that the lexicon contains no Korean morphemes. Typological comparisons offered were Korean/Japanese/Turkic head-finality, Quechua/Tibetan/Turkic evidentials and Papuan/Austronesian quinary numerals — all statements about type, none about vocabulary. The priors accepted in D52 continue to hold on this surface.

## Verdict

**Stone → English passes at 121/121 meaning-correct. English → stone does not clearly clear the bar: 109/121 = 90.1% as scored, but 107/119 = 89.9% once the two leaked items are discounted, against a ≥90% bar.**

Say it plainly: the bar is not met. The scored number reaches it only on the strength of two held sentences that were copies of train sentences, and a number two invalid items produced is not a measurement. Every miss is well formed and every miss preserves meaning, which is a real and large improvement, but the exact-match bar itself is still one or two sentences short.

The margin should be read against what caused it. Three of the twelve misses are the residue of D78 being applied to fourteen lines and not to fifteen more; had it been carried through, the same answers would have scored 110/119 = 92.4% on the valid items. That is arithmetic, not a measurement. The corrections in this session make the next run the measurement, and this is the second run in a row whose result turns on corpus defects rather than on what the model can do.

What is not arithmetic is the change in kind. Test 3's misses included four real model failures and three meaning changes; test 4's include neither. The corpus is now the thing deciding the answers, which is what the acceptance test was built to find out.

Actions taken this session, all after scoring:
1. **D87** — the fifteen remaining D78 violations corrected, and the validator's convention heuristic rewritten so it can actually see them. Baseline is now 0 and 1.
2. **D88** — first-person intend takes the future marker; predict takes the inferred evidential. Four lines corrected.
3. **D89** — G29 closed: alternative questions coordinate the smallest differing constituent. Two lines corrected.
4. **D90** — the say/tell root is the only root for saying. Two lines corrected.
5. **D91** — the cue rule extended to every evidential; four English sides corrected to say what their scripts say.
6. **D92** — seventeen train sentences for the three thin constructions. No new roots.
7. **D93** — the two leaked held sentences corrected, plus one train sentence added this session that duplicated a held script; `validate.py` now fails on any held line whose script or English is verbatim in train.

8. **D94** — four static checks, because eleven of these twelve misses were findable by reading the corpus and this run cost ~683k subagent tokens to find them. Three are errors (the two D88 pairings, the D84/D91 cue rule); one is a report (English words naming more than one predicate root). Regression-tested: 7 of 7 on the pre-correction forms. It immediately caught a latent "the variable" collision and an error in one of this session's own new sentences.

Thirty-three corpus lines corrected, seventeen added. Corpus 1009, held 121, lexicon 206, 0 validator errors.

## What this run says about the next one

Do not re-run this held set. Fourteen of its 121 sentences have now been edited in response to a test result — four after test 3, ten after test 4 — so a re-run would measure a corpus fitted to the test, and the number would rise while meaning less. The next acceptance run needs a **fresh held set**: sentences no test has seen and none edited in response to one.

And it should be spent on the question only it can answer. Going back through the twelve misses, eleven were findable by static audit of the corpus; only s0431 needed judgement, and even there the decisive fact is a count. The checks added under D94 now cover those classes at zero model cost. The acceptance test is for whether a cold reader can reconstruct the language — not for linting the corpus.
