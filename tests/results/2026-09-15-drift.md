# Drift test 1 — 2026-09-15, the first run of D15

Corpus s0001–s0865 (the run predates the 24 sentences added the same day), lexicon 196 entries. Instrument `scripts/drift_test.py`, protocol D74, model `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5` over OpenRouter at temperature 0. Materials given in full per the translation protocol (D72): grammar, the whole lexicon, 250 train sentences.

Two runs: the long session (42 turns — probes, 40 filler turns, probes again) and the control (the same 40 filler sentences, each in its own fresh one-turn conversation).

**Honest note on the protocol.** The protocol and the twelve probes were fixed before the first run. The control arm was not: it was added after the first run, because the first run's numbers turned out to be unreadable without it (see signal 2). The protocol in `tests/README.md` now requires both arms.

## Signal 1 — probe divergence: 0 of 12

Every one of the twelve probe answers is byte-identical at turn 1 and at turn 42, including the two that are wrong (below). Necessary but not sufficient: the model can see its own turn-1 answers when it answers at turn 42, so this cannot by itself distinguish stability from copying. That is what signals 2 and 3 are for.

## Signal 2 — filler accuracy, long session against control: no drift

| | first 20 turns | last 20 turns | total | well formed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| long session | 20/20 | 8/20 | **28/40** | 40/40 |
| control (fresh session each) | 19/20 | 5/20 | **24/40** | 35/40 |

Read the long session alone and it looks like heavy drift: 20/20 collapsing to 8/20. It is not drift. The filler sentences are drawn in corpus order, so the second half is the newer and less consistent corpus — the same region that carried most of the misses in acceptance test 3 the same day — and **the control drops further, to 5/20.**

Per sentence: identical output in both runs on 26 of 40. The long session and the control disagree on 4 sentences, and **the long session is the one that is right in all 4** (s0389, s0450, s0756, s0797); there is no sentence the control got right and the long session got wrong. Well-formedness goes the same way, 40/40 against 35/40 — one control answer was not a translation at all but the opening of a reasoning preamble.

So on this evidence a long session makes output **more** faithful, not less. Accumulated context is working as context, and 40 turns of unrelated work in the language did not erode it.

## Signal 3 — Korean leakage: none

289 tokens emitted across the long session, 172 across the control. Zero tokens that are real Korean words and that the parser cannot read as words of this language. The specific failure D52 left open did not occur.

One token did trip the naive form of the check and is worth recording, because it will trip it again: the failure root plus the general evidential is also a Korean word of frequency 11. It is a perfectly legal form of this language, so the check filters through the parser first and only counts tokens the parser rejects. The Hangul priors of D52 survive this run, on both surfaces tested today.

## What the run did find: three stable errors, present at turn 1 and unchanged at turn 42

These are not drift — they do not appear or worsen over the session — but the instrument surfaced them and they are real.

1. **Two stance markers stacked.** p09 came back as a predicate carrying both the intend and the risk stance. There is one stance slot (D34), so the validator rejects it: caught, both times.
2. **A non-word.** p10 came back with a one-vowel error in the plan root, making a syllable pair that is not a word. Caught by the validator, both times.
3. **A modal with no subordinator, and the validator let it through.** p11 and p12 put a bare root directly before the modal instead of the subordinated complement D61 and D63 require. The parser reads a root before a root as a determiner or compound (D44, D48), so the sentence is *well formed* and means something else — modal-ability-of-fixing rather than being able to fix. Five of the twelve second-half filler misses are the same error. This is a hole in the guardrail, not in the language, and it is the one finding from this run that changed the tooling: `translate.py check` now prints an advisory when a modal follows a bare root (D76). It stays an advisory and not a validator error because the corpus has one legitimate modal with a plain object (asking what someone wants).

## Verdict

**No drift, on all three signals, and the answer to the question D52 left open is no:** output did not slide toward Korean over a long session, and did not degrade in any measurable way. The apparent collapse in the second half is corpus difficulty, and the control run is what proves it — without that arm this run would have reported a large drift that is not there.

Two things follow for the method. First, the control arm is now part of the protocol; a drift run without it is not interpretable. Second, the second-half difficulty is the same signal acceptance test 3 gave from a different direction, and it points at the same place: the newest region of the corpus (s0598 onward) is where the language is least settled.
