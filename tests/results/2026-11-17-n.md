The first general sealed set (`tests/fixtures/roundtrip_test_2026-11-17.txt`, 80 lines) was retired and read, on the maintainer's instruction, after D137 was committed and measured. Each item was read on the D137 corpus (1496 lines), with its stone and both pairs' back-translations: `retired-2026-11-17/focus_test.json`. A fresh sealed set replaces it, written before this reading began.

**Missing words are 38 of the 55 items that are not clearly fine.** The pattern is D132's: the sender does not drop the unnameable noun, it **substitutes the nearest root, and the result reads as complete**.

## Three substitution sinks
1. **The variable/store root.** It absorbed every storage location: *archive, shared drive, network drive, repo* (twice). It appeared in 5 back-translations whose original had no variable, and all 5 were storage places. Its gloss is "variable, store" and its notes say "a store … to store". The sender reaches for *store*, and the receiver reads *variable*. **This is a carve defect, not a vocabulary gap.** One form spans a program's slot and a place data is kept. It is the most specific fixable finding here.
2. **The place root.** It appeared in 12 back-translations whose original lacked *place*: path, archive, drive, layout, screen, spam, dock. *Path* is the worst case, failing in 3 of 3 lines (#9, #11, #68). A path is the most common noun in the D130 code-request domain after *file*, and it has no word.
3. **The loop/repeat root.** It took *auto-refresh* and *units are off*, in 3 back-translations.

## What it says about the open decisions
- **Quoting (decision 3).** 3 of 80 ordinary lines (4%) need a literal. Both senders improvised. One wrote Latin `PDF`/`PNG` inside the stone line, and that pair scored FAITHFUL. The other spelled *Python* in Hangul outside the 74 syllables. **The senders are inventing quoting already. The question is whether the language specifies it or leaves each sender to improvise.**
- **Short answers (decision 2).** "Yes, and …" was rendered as the true/hold root with the direct evidential in three lines, and came back as *it exists* or *that holds*. "No, only on the desktop version" failed outright: the *no* answers a question, and the sender negated the clause instead.
- **Focus words (D137).** They carried in every line that had one: *only moved it*, *also in the network program*, *test it too*. Every one of those lines failed on a noun.

## Parse rate
5 of 160 stone outputs fail the parser. Senders mostly stay inside the language. The losses are in meaning, not form.
