# Adversarial corpus review, and a free-text round trip — 2026-10-09

Corpus 1477, lexicon 230, at `fd08821`. Run because the maintainer reported that a
live hand-run conversation failed completely while the repo's own tests report ~90%.

**Result: the maintainer is right and the tests have been measuring the wrong thing.
Three independent models, given the whole corpus and asked to attack it, converge on
the same explanation — and five of their specific claims were checked and all five are
true. One of them reverses a decision made yesterday.**

## What was run

**Round trip, free text.** 20 sentences an ordinary person would say, none from the
corpus (*"It worked yesterday and now it doesn't"*, *"Sorry, I'm not following"*).
English → stone by `gemini-3.8-flash`, stone → English by `grok-4.6`, which never saw
the originals. Raw in `tests/fixtures/roundtrip_2026-10-09.json`.

**Adversarial review.** `gemini-3.8-flash`, `grok-4.6`, `z-ai/glm-5.3`, each given the
grammar, the full lexicon with sense notes and all 1477 lines (~50k tokens in), told
the 90%/failure discrepancy and asked to explain it. No praise, findings only, cite
ids. Raw in `tests/fixtures/corpus_review_2026-10-09.json`.

## The round trip says the language is not broken

9 faithful, 6 acceptable with loss, 3 degraded, **2 broken** — 15 of 20 usable. So the
*total* failure the maintainer hit was probably the harness (a Custom Gem adding its
own instructions, an attachment retrieved rather than read, a model drifting from
interpreting into conversing), not the language refusing to carry meaning.

The two breaks are diagnostic. *"Is there a way to do this without breaking the other
thing?"* came back as *"If that does not fail, can this be done?"* — a constraint
turned into a condition. *"Sorry, I'm not following"* came back as *"I made a mistake;
I don't understand it"* — phatic apology read as an admission of fault.

**And a gap no earlier test found: focus particles.** *only, else, other, just, even,
instead* — **not one has a root.** *"It only happens when I open the big one"* →
*"It fails when I open the big thing."* The word carrying the whole diagnosis is
dropped silently. D122 filed *even* and *especially* under quantitative hedging beside
*almost*; that was the wrong bucket. These do not scale a quantity, they **select among
alternatives**, which is the core move of a debugging conversation.

## Why the tests look fine — all three reviewers, independently

- **gemini**: "isolated template matching against a synthetic corpus… a combinatorial
  permutation grid, not natural language."
- **grok**: "template fill on i.i.d. clones of this corpus… That is retrieval."
- **glm**: "The 90% measures template completion, i.e. corpus self-consistency. A test
  drawn from the corpus cannot detect what the corpus cannot say."

**And all three independently name the same mechanism, which is the hard part to hear:
the English side carries the answer.** The cue rule — D79, D84, D91, D94, D105, enforced
by the validator — requires every marked evidential and stance to show an English cue.
*"I'm told"* → `케`. *"Everyone knows that"* → `코`. *"I insist"* → `무`. grok: "The
model is translating glosses, not meaning. Strip those crutches (as a user does) and
the mapping is underdetermined — which is exactly when use fails." **The rule that made
the corpus well-formed is the rule that makes the benchmark trivial**, and it was added
to stop the acceptance test being undetermined. It worked, by removing the task.

## Five checkable claims, all verified

1. **Three byte-identical duplicate pairs**: s0855/s0953, s0938/s1160, s0942/s1055.
   The D83 check looks for one English with two scripts and cannot see a full duplicate.
   All six are currently `train`, so nothing leaked — but D93's guarantee does not cover
   this shape.
2. **The corpus asks questions it cannot answer.** s0190 *what is the file's name*,
   s0284 *the variable's name*, s0317 *what did the machine say*, s1278 *what is the
   failure message*, s1462 *the package's version* — and **s0069 `에 왜 파 요카마`,
   "Name the file", an imperative the language cannot obey.**
3. **70 of 1477 sentences put the direct evidential on an unwitnessed future** —
   `라투카마`, "I'll fix it, witnessed". A logical incoherence in 5% of the corpus.
4. **D117 is contradicted by the corpus 56 to 0.** It states that a subordinate clause
   restates a shared subject. On modal constructions, where the subject is necessarily
   shared, **56 sentences drop it and none restate it.** D117 was decided on "2 to 1"
   evidence and says of itself "unchecked, because an absent subject cannot be told from
   a legitimately agentless embedded clause."
5. **No dialogue mechanics at all.** 1477 standalone lines, zero multi-turn exchanges,
   zero elliptical answers. Because the evidential is obligatory on every predicate, a
   polar question can only be answered by rebuilding the whole clause *and* choosing an
   epistemic source. gemini: *"Real-time dialogue collapses under the cognitive overhead
   of generating complete transitive clauses for simple conversational turns."*

## What this reverses

**D133 declared quoting out of scope, yesterday.** Claim 2 kills that: the corpus itself
contains six lines that require naming, including a command it cannot obey. Quoting is
not an optional extra for an unclaimed register — **the language already asks for it and
cannot answer itself.**

## Verdict

1. The language carries ordinary meaning at roughly 15 of 20 on free text. It is not
   broken, and the live total failure was most likely harness.
2. **The acceptance test measures corpus self-consistency, not communication**, and the
   cue rule is the specific mechanism that makes it so. Every number from test 3 onward
   should be read that way.
3. Four concrete defects to fix: the duplicates, the future/direct incoherence, D117,
   and focus particles.
4. Quoting is back in scope.
