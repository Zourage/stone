# D131 measured — a controlled before/after — 2026-09-19

One sentence, three models, two corpora differing **only** by D131's 6 roots and
35 sentences: `corpus.jsonl` at `3e0e659` (1434 lines) against the current 1469.
Raw output in `tests/fixtures/d131_ab_2026-09-19.txt`. **Serves goal 1** (D125).

Input, the maintainer's own failing sentence: **"Build me a script for a shell container."**

| | BEFORE (1434) | AFTER (1469) |
|---|---|---|
| stone | `에 키포 헤 워 파 아 헤 리카마` | `에 세쿠 파 다내 후태 체데 후태 헤 아 페 리카마` |
| cold read | **"Build the code for the variable for me."** | **"Build the program in the shell and the container for me."** |
| receiver asks | *What is the variable's name?* | *Where should I build it?* |

Translator `gemini-3.8-flash`, cold reader `grok-4.6` (no context, no brief),
receiver `glm-5.3` asked for one clarifying question.

## The before-state was worse than "vague"

D131 predicted the sentence would render as *build me a thing*. **It did not.** The
translator **substituted** the nearest available nouns — the variable root for
*container*, the write root for *script* — and produced a fluent, well-formed,
**confidently wrong** sentence naming concepts that were never in the request. The
receiver then asked a precise, useful-sounding question about an object that does
not exist.

**Omission would have been safer.** A sentence that dropped the unsayable nouns
would at least read as underspecified. Substitution reads as complete. **This is the
sharpest form of the silent-loss failure and it was not anticipated.**

## What D131 fixed

Both taught compounds were produced unprompted and recovered by a cold reader that
had never been told what they mean: `다내 후태` shell, `체데 후태` container. The
right things are now named, and the clarifying question moved from an invented
object to a real missing parameter.

## What it exposed: G36, no way to bracket

`다내 후태 체데 후태` came back as *"the shell **and** the container"* — two places
in a list, not one *shell container*. **Two adjacent compounds flatten.**

The corpus has runs of three to five bare roots (76, 10 and 3 sentences), and
**every one is flat**: determiner or numeral stacking before a single head, as in
`니포 야 수포 메루 카유 페`. **There is no attested case of a two-root compound
modifying another two-root compound, and the grammar says nothing about how one
would be read.**

This is a gap D131 *created*. Before it, two-root compounds were rare enough that
abutting ones never arose; D130 and D131 added twelve, and four-root strings are now
easy to produce and undecidable to read. Logged as **G36**.

## Verdict

D131 cleared the block it aimed at. **The blocker moved rather than vanished** —
three things now stand between this and a usable code conversation: quoting (the
receiver can ask for a name and cannot be told one), G36 bracketing, and the sixteen
domain nouns not coined.

One sentence, one run. It shows a block cleared; it does not show the domain covered.

## A methodological note on this run

An earlier report of this result quoted *"Build the program into a shell container
for me"* as the cold read. **That was the receiver model's own gloss**, taken from a
truncated capture, not the independent reader's. The cold reader said *"in the shell
and the container"*, which is the finding above. **A model's description of what it
understood is not a measurement of what it understood** — that is exactly what the
cold reader exists to prevent, and the error was made anyway by reading the wrong
line out of the output.
