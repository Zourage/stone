# Acceptance test 3 — 2026-09-15, the full held set

Corpus s0001–s0865, 758 train, **107 held**. Lexicon 196 entries. Script Hangul.
Bar (D50): stone → English 100% meaning-correct, English → stone ≥90% exact, every miss read by hand and grammatical.

## Protocol, and one change to it (D75)

Six cold agents, each given the 758 train sentences as `English ||| the language` pairs and nothing else — no spec, no lexicon, no grammar — and asked for the translations, the rules it inferred, and whether the language is a known one. Each agent's only tool call was one read of its own self-contained prompt file outside the repo; each confirmed in its report that it never touched the repo.

The change: **the two directions were run by different agents.** Tests 1 and 2 gave one agent both sides of every held sentence, so the answer to each English → stone item was sitting in the same prompt as its stone → English item. That leak is why the split was made: three agents got only the stone side of a third of the held set each, three got only the English side. Every held sentence still gets both directions; no agent ever sees both sides of one sentence. 36 / 36 / 35.

## Stone → English: 107/107 meaning-correct — passes

Read by hand against the corpus English. 68 of 107 came back word-identical; the other 39 differ only in contraction, article, number-neutral rendering, or which of the fixed cues was used for the same evidential or stance ("must be faulty" vs "is faulty, I gather"; "might go wrong" vs "might break something"; "loop" vs "repeat" for the one root that is both). None changed a meaning.

Four of the differences are the agent being **more** faithful to the script than the corpus English is, and each one flags a defect on the English side (see class B and C below): s0163, s0455 and s0835 came back carrying the general or predict cue that their script marks and their corpus English omits, and s0852 came back as "it happens often" where its corpus English says "it fails often" although the script has no failure root in it.

## English → stone: 87/107 exact = 81.3% — fails

Per chunk: 35/36, 28/36, 24/35. The drop is monotonic in corpus age: the misses concentrate in s0598–s0864, the newest region (D66–D70).

All 20 misses parse as well formed under `scripts/validate.py`. 17 of 20 preserve the meaning; 3 change the evidential, and those 3 are the class where the corpus contradicts itself.

### Class A — where a clause-initial adverbial goes relative to the subject (6 misses)
s0522, s0669, s0708, s0746, s0831, s0848.

In each, the agent put the bare pronoun subject first and the corpus puts the fronted time/place/subordinate phrase first, or the reverse. Example, s0669: gold `니로 카유 페 우 누타카`, agent `우 니로 카유 페 누타카`.

**The corpus teaches both orders and the spec does not decide.** D33 says only "subject, object, verb". Train has 22 sentences with a fronted adverbial before a bare pronoun subject and 90 with the subject first, and it contains a near-duplicate pair in direct conflict: train s0857 `에 와 초코디 유 히 로헤카마` and held s0708 `와 초코디 유 히 에 로헤카마` are the same construction with the subject on opposite sides. Train s0857 against train s0858 `우 쿠테타코디 유 히 아 로헤타카` is the same conflict inside the train split. The agent followed s0857 and was marked wrong by s0708.

This is the single largest cause of failure and it is a gap in the spec, not in the model. Logged as **G27**.

### Class B — the evidential on a generic statement whose English carries no cue (3 misses)
s0163 (gold general, agent direct), s0835 (gold general, agent direct), s0547 (gold **direct**, agent general).

`corpus/README.md` says "a sentence whose English has no cue is direct". **The train split breaks its own rule.** 21 of the 52 general-evidential sentences carry no cue in their English; the conditionals among them are fine, since the general evidential there follows from the irrealis rule, but the gnomic ones are not: train s0029 "Faults get fixed.", s0059 "A result is a number.", s0483 "Every function can fail.", s0714 "Every run takes time." and s0793 are all general with nothing in the English to say so.

So the train split teaches that a cue-less gnomic statement may be general, and the convention says it must be direct. The three misses are that contradiction cashing out in both directions: on s0163 and s0835 the gold is general and the agent wrote direct; on s0547 the gold is direct and the agent, having learned the gnomic pattern from train, wrote general.

These are the only misses that change meaning, and the corpus cannot say which reading is right. Logged as **G28**.

### Class C — defects in the held sentences themselves (2 further misses, 3 defects)
- **s0750** "I was blocked, but I did not tell you." Gold marks the addressee with the object case; **D67 decided that the addressee of say/tell takes the location case**, and all 15 train sentences with that root use the location case. The agent wrote the location case and was marked wrong. Gold is wrong.
- **s0860** "Since the version is new, it is fast." The English "since" here is causal, and gold renders it with the *temporal* since-construction (clause + time + after, D70). The agent used the `from` word, which is the decided form for because (D69). The agent is right about the English; gold reads it as temporal. (Also the only held use of a construction with one train example.)
- **s0746** "When will it be ready?" Gold has no future marker although the English says "will"; the agent supplied it. Train s0046 `에 우 파 야 유 페 다투 추` keeps the future marker in exactly this shape. Gold is missing a morpheme. (Counted under class A for its word order.)

### Class D — thin train coverage (4 misses)
- **s0517** we-exclusive, **zero train sentences** — the only sentence in the corpus with that feature, and it is held. The agent wrote `아 우 예 하 …` for gold `아 우 하 …`. An unambiguous corpus gap.
- **s0864** region nominals, 4 train sentences. Agent used possessor + the exist root where gold compounds and uses the place root; both patterns are attested under D70.
- **s0852** how-often, 3 train sentences. Agent made the failure root the subject where gold uses the pronoun.
- **s0629** clause as subject, 2 train sentences. Agent dropped the generic person root that gold uses as the subject of the nominalised clause. Its twin s0835 has no such subject in gold, so the corpus shows both.

### Class E — attested both ways, gold took the minority (1 miss)
**s0551** "I'll do it; it will probably be fast." Gold `파로카마` has no future marker on the intend-stance predicate; train has 114 intend predicates without it and 28 with, so both are licensed. Gold's second predicate pairs predict with the **direct** evidential; train pairs predict with the inferred evidential 13 times and with direct once. The agent followed the majority on both counts and lost the exact match to the minority.

### Class F — genuine model choices, grammatical and meaning-preserving (4 misses)
s0412 (took "it" rather than "the run" as the object of the repeat root), s0431 (reduced the alternative question to one clause, on the model of the reduced alternatives the train split does show, where gold repeats the whole clause), s0475 (direct rather than inferred on a negated modal), s0529 (anaphoric pronoun where gold repeats the noun). These four are the real score.

### What the score would be
Classes A, B and C-unique are 11 of the 20 misses, and all 11 are the corpus failing to decide something or contradicting itself. Decide G27 and G28 and correct the three defective held sentences, and **the same answers score 98/107 = 91.6%**, above the bar. The remaining 9 split into 4 real misses, 4 thin-coverage misses and 1 minority-form miss.

## Rules the agents inferred

Complete and correct, in all six independent write-ups. Between them: SOV and head-final throughout; the three case markers and the relational words, including the `from` word doing ablative, comparison and cause, and the `with` word doing comitative, instrument, the second term of a symmetric relation, and manner; determiners and compounds by bare juxtaposition; numeral before a root as a count and after it as a label; the verb template root–negation–tense–evidential–stance–subordinator with the right gloss for every one of the four evidentials and all six stances; no copula; the imperative as second person plus intend; the agentless clause keeping the object marker; the question particle with the evidential slot empty; the whole interrogative series off one root; the general evidential in conditional protases; the `general + subordinator` form as the complement of all four modals and as the base of while, until, since, because, become and still; the negative determiner carrying the negation alone; quinary 6–9 inside a decimal system with additive composition; comparison with the `from` word; "we" as pronoun plus pronoun plus `with`.

Nothing was inferred wrongly. Two agents independently flagged the same residual puzzle, and they are right about it: train **s0532** "My function is not faster than yours" is written as a double-negated time periphrasis rather than as the decided comparison pattern (the `from` word plus a stative), and is opaque. A third corpus defect to correct.

## Priors check (D52)

All six agents were asked directly. All six said constructed, not a known language, and all six named the script as the only real-world element. None translated any word by its Korean reading, and several went out of their way to rule out the near-misses: that the numerals do not line up with Korean, that the case markers are not the Korean particles, that the machine root is not the Korean word. Three named one or two syllables where they would not rule out a deliberate nod. The priors accepted in D52 hold on this surface; the drift test (same date, `2026-09-15-drift.md`) is the instrument for the other one.

## Verdict

**Stone → English passes at 107/107. English → stone fails at 81.3% against the ≥90% bar.**

Not a failure of the language. 11 of the 20 misses are the corpus declining to decide a point (G27, G28) or contradicting itself, and 4 more are constructions with two or fewer train sentences. Actions, in order:

1. Decide **G27** (position of a clause-initial adverbial relative to the subject) and **G28** (the evidential on a cue-less generic statement). Both need a decision before the next run; either resolution rewrites existing corpus lines, so it is a maintainer call, not a test-run call.
2. Correct **s0750** (addressee case, against D67), **s0746** (missing future marker), **s0860** (causal since written as temporal since) and **s0532** (the comparison periphrasis). There is no route in the tooling for correcting an existing corpus line — `merge_batch.py` only appends — which is itself an open item (**O8**).
3. Train sentences added this session for the thin constructions: we-exclusive, which had none, plus region nominals, how-often, clause-as-subject and the causal/temporal since contrast. See the batch note in `decisions.md` (D76).
