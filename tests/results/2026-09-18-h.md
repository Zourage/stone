# Acceptance test 8 — cross-model — 2026-09-18

Corpus 1424 sentences, lexicon 224 entries, at `109d80d`. **Serves goal 1** (D125).

**Result: the language is learnable across model families, and the ~90% figure is
not a Claude artifact — Claude came third of five.** Three of five models clear
D50's ≥90% exact bar on English → stone; all five are essentially 100%
meaning-correct on stone → English. **Two corpus defects and one thin
construction were found by cross-model agreement, none of which seven
Claude-only runs had surfaced.**

## Why this run exists

Every one of tests 1–7 used Claude subagents. What they establish is *Claude can
translate this language*, and goal 1 says *an LLM*. Those are different claims,
and the difference bites hardest on exactly the dimension this project has been
most careful about: D52's concern is Hangul priors bleeding in, and priors are
model-specific.

## Protocol

Same as the acceptance test, through the OpenRouter route `translate.py` already
uses, with two changes that make it cheaper and stricter rather than looser.

- **Fresh virtual draw.** 60 held sentences sampled from the 1401 whose script
  and English are both unique in the corpus, so D93 validity holds by
  construction. The corpus is **not modified** — the sentences are simply held
  out of the context. This also avoids test 7's held set, which had ten
  corrected lines and was no longer clean. Eight of the 60 come from the
  D123/D124 additions, so the new roots are exercised.
- **Directions split (D75), four stateless calls per model.** Two chunks of 30;
  each chunk asked in both directions but never in the same call, so no call
  sees both sides of any held sentence.
- Context is the other 1364 pairs as `English ||| script`, ~21k tokens. No spec,
  no lexicon, no grammar.
- Scoring: English → stone is exact match, the half D50's bar is set on.
  Stone → English is screened on content-word overlap and every line below the
  screen is read by hand, because the bar there is meaning, not wording.

The stone → English column is a hand-read verdict. The mechanical screen flagged

*it got late*, *all the lines are blue* against *every line is blue*, *twice a
week* against *two times a week*. Two are not paraphrase and both are discussed
below.

## What cross-model agreement found that seven Claude runs did not

**s0103 is wrong.** "Twenty-six files" is `네 푸 포 나 왜 얘카` — two-ten-five-one.
**All five models wrote `포나` instead**, which is the lexicon's own root for
*six* (w0063 family, D48). The corpus line contradicts the lexicon it was built
from. Worse in the other direction: Claude and Grok could not read it at all,
producing "there are two, ten, five, or one files". GLM read it correctly as 26.
A line five independent model families all reject the same way is a defect, not
five slips.

**s0738 is wrong too, and Gemini produced the right answer.** "Which task has
priority?" is `야 사세 소패 얘 추`. All five models inserted the possessor: `야
사세 피 소패 …`. They are right. s0093 teaches having as possessor + the hold
root — `아 피 나 왜 얘카`, "I have one file" — and s0738 drops the possessor while
using the same hold root. Gemini's `야 사세 피 소패 얘 추` is what the corpus's own
pattern gives.

**The since-clause is thin, not broken.** Four of five fronted the because-clause
in s0888 ahead of the subject, violating D102. The rule is unanimous in the
corpus and the check enforces it — but D103 counted the until/since clause at
**0–2**, two train examples total. Four models failing the same construction is a
coverage floor problem, not a rule problem.

## Minimal-pair substitutions — and they reverse part of D125

Four of the 33 misses across all five models are one root written where a
one-syllable neighbour was wanted:

| model | wrote | wanted |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-5.6-luna | 예피 *drink* | 예페 *eat* |
| glm-5.3 | 캐포 *minute* | 케포 *message* |
| claude-sonnet-4.5 | 사해 *effort* | 사세 *task* |
| claude-sonnet-4.5 | 사세 *task* | 레세 *be responsible for* |

**D125 stood down the recoining of the D123–D124 roots on the reasoning that "a
script reads exact tokens and so does an LLM, and neither mishears a vowel."
That is wrong on the generation side and this run is the counterexample.** A
model reading is not the issue; a model *writing* selects from a neighbourhood,
and a denser neighbourhood is more chances to select wrong. One of the four is
`예피`/`예페` — both coined in D124, both in the person family, one vowel apart,
and the confusion cost Luna a held sentence.

12% of all misses in this run are this class.

## Verdict

1. **Goal 1's claim generalises.** Three of five model families clear the bar and
   Claude is third. The result was never a Claude artifact.
2. **Two models do not clear it** — GLM-5.3 at 85.0% and GPT-5.6 Luna at 78.3%.
   The bar is met by some LLMs, not by every LLM, and the goal should say which.
3. **Cross-model disagreement is a corpus-defect detector**, and a cheap one.
   Five families rejecting a line the same way found two defects that seven
   Claude-only runs never surfaced. Worth running again for that reason alone.
4. **The minimal-pair density defect costs goal 1**, contrary to D125.

Scoring both directions here is exact-match plus a hand-read. The 60-sentence
draw is smaller than tests 3–7's 107–125, so a single item is 1.7 points; treat
the ordering as solid and the individual percentages as ±2.
